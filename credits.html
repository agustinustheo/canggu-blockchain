Canggu on the Polkadot SDK

Credits and authorship

This project stands on other people's work. This page says exactly whose.

The idea: Canggu

Canggu, its name, its design and its original whitepaper (v1.3, March 2025) are the work of:

  • Dendi Suhubdy (Bitwyre)
  • Tejveer Singh (Bitwyre)
  • Mitul Dhawan (Bitwyre)
  • Maldini Sonkeng (Bitwyre)

They published the design and the start of an implementation as open source under the GPL-3.0 license. The first commits of the code repository are theirs (by Dendi Suhubdy and Mitul Dhawan, @mitul72). The whitepaper repository's history is theirs (by Dendi Suhubdy and Maldini Sonkeng). Both histories are kept unchanged in the forks this book links to. Credit for the idea belongs to them.

This rebuild is not endorsed by them. It changes and corrects parts of their design (see What the original design got wrong). Any mistakes in the rebuild are ours, not theirs.

The rebuild: who did what

  • Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, wrote the code, the tests, the measurements, the whitepaper v2.0 and this book. That includes the parts built by separate Claude sessions working on sub-tasks (the Metal GPU kernels and the SP1 programs).
  • agustinustheo started the project, chose the goals, pointed to the sources (Canggu, Rand Protocol, SP1, verifiable), reviewed the results and published them under his GitHub account. In his own words, it is not his work.

The goal was research: to learn how far the Polkadot SDK can be pushed toward a design this ambitious. It is not a product and has not been audited.

Ideas we adapted

  • Rand Protocol, a later blockchain by Dendi Suhubdy (full node), described in the whitepaper Randprotocol: Confidential Arbitrary Computations by Dendi Suhubdy (Bitwyre) and Anish Mohammed (Zeroknowledge Consulting Ltd) (PDF). Sealed ordering adapts its corrected front-running theorem (Proposition 14.2 and Theorem 15.4). The signature-compression analysis follows its block-aggregation and block-space documents.
  • MiMC hash design by Martin Albrecht, Lorenzo Grassi, Christian Rechberger, Arnab Roy and Tyge Tiessen (used in the demo Groth16 circuit).
  • Groth16 proof system by Jens Groth.
  • CRYSTALS-Dilithium / ML-DSA by the CRYSTALS team, standardised by NIST as FIPS 204.
