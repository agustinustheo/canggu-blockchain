Canggu on the Polkadot SDK

What Canggu set out to do

Canggu is a blockchain design published in March 2025 by Dendi Suhubdy, Tejveer Singh, Mitul Dhawan and Maldini Sonkeng (Bitwyre) in the whitepaper "Canggu Blockchain: A Quantum-Secure, MEV-Resistant Blockchain for Parallel Computing" (v1.3). This page summarises their goals. Later chapters show how each one was built, and where the design had to change.

The four problems it targets

  1. Quantum computers. The signatures that protect almost every blockchain account today (ECDSA, Ed25519, Sr25519) can be broken by a large enough quantum computer running Shor's algorithm. Canggu wants accounts that stay safe.
  2. Front-running (MEV). Whoever builds a block chooses the order of its transactions. They can put their own trade just before yours and profit from it. Canggu wants ordering that block producers cannot exploit.
  3. Sequential execution. Most chains run transactions one at a time on one CPU core, even when the transactions do not touch the same data. Canggu wants to use parallel hardware, including GPUs.
  4. Slow or fragile consensus. Canggu wants fast block production with a rotating leader and finality in under a second.

The five building blocks it proposes

Building block (v1.3)In plain words
CRYSTALS-Dilithium signatures, Kyber key exchange, XMSS hashingUse the new NIST post-quantum algorithms everywhere
A DAG-based mempoolTransactions point at the transactions they depend on, so they cannot be reordered past them
Leader-based BFT consensus (like Solana's Tower BFT)One leader at a time proposes blocks; a vote finalises them
ZK-SNARKs for verifiers and proversExpensive work is proven once, and everyone else checks a small proof
Parallel smart contracts on CPU and GPUContracts can include GPU code (CUDA) for data-parallel work

The whitepaper also targets 50,000+ transactions per second.

What this rebuild found

Most of the design could be built, sometimes much more easily than expected. Several claims did not survive contact with real code and measurements: for example, Kyber cannot sign transactions, BLS12-381 proofs are not quantum-safe, and 50,000 transactions per second does not fit with post-quantum signature sizes. Those findings are not criticism of the authors' ambition; they are what an implementation is for. See What the original design got wrong.

The original whitepaper is kept unchanged at canggu-blockchain-whitepaper (main.tex and Canggu_Blockchain_Whitepaper_v1_3.pdf). The Polkadot SDK edition (v2.0), which describes this code, is in the same repository under v2/.
