Post-quantum cryptography and zero knowledge, briefly

Two kinds of cryptography appear everywhere in this book. You do not need the mathematics; you need to know what each one promises and what it costs.

Post-quantum signatures

A digital signature proves that the owner of a private key approved a message. Today's blockchain signatures (ECDSA, Ed25519, Sr25519) rest on elliptic-curve problems. A large quantum computer running Shor's algorithm would solve those problems and forge signatures.

In 2024 NIST standardised replacements that are believed to resist quantum computers:

StandardOld nameWhat it isUsed in Canggu
FIPS 204, ML-DSACRYSTALS-DilithiumSignature scheme (lattice-based)Yes: ML-DSA-65 for every account
FIPS 203, ML-KEMCRYSTALS-KyberKey exchange, not a signatureNo (it cannot sign transactions)
FIPS 205, SLH-DSASPHINCS+Signature scheme (hash-based)No (a conservative alternative)

The cost is size. An ML-DSA-65 public key is 1,952 bytes and a signature 3,309 bytes. A classic Sr25519 public key and signature are 32 and 64 bytes. That size difference drives several results in this book.

Hash functions (like BLAKE2b, which Polkadot SDK chains use) are much less affected: a quantum computer only halves their security, so a 256-bit hash keeps about 128 bits of security. That is enough.

Zero-knowledge proofs

A zero-knowledge proof lets someone prove a statement is true without revealing why. For example: "these 16 salaries, which I will not show you, add up to 50,000". Two properties matter for blockchains:

  • Succinct: checking the proof is fast and small, no matter how much work it proves.
  • Zero knowledge: the proof reveals nothing beyond the statement.

Making the proof (proving) is slow and expensive. Checking it (verifying) is fast. That asymmetry is the whole point: one machine proves, every validator verifies.

What a zkVM adds

Writing a proof "circuit" by hand is hard. A zkVM (zero-knowledge virtual machine) proves that an ordinary program ran correctly. SP1, the zkVM used here, proves Rust programs compiled to RISC-V. In Canggu, SP1 proves two programs:

  • a payroll check (the quantum-safe version of the Groth16 demo), and
  • a program that verifies a whole batch of ML-DSA signatures, so the chain can accept the proof instead of the signatures (signature compression).

The Canggu chain does not run a zkVM itself. It verifies zkVM proofs that are made off-chain.
