Canggu on the Polkadot SDK

Lessons from Rand Protocol

Rand Protocol is a later blockchain by Dendi Suhubdy, one of Canggu's original authors. Its whitepaper, Randprotocol: Confidential Arbitrary Computations, is by Dendi Suhubdy and Anish Mohammed. It makes many of the same choices (Dilithium, STARK proofs, GPU provers) on a different foundation. Comparing the two explains a lot about both.

One decision explains most differences

Rand is a fully shielded chain: no accounts, no balances, no visible senders. Canggu, like every Polkadot SDK chain, is an account chain.

What we took

  1. The corrected front-running theorem (Proposition 14.2 and Theorem 15.4), adapted with hash commitments instead of threshold encryption. See Sealed ordering.
  2. The signature-compression analysis. Rand's documents give the architecture (pipelined aggregation over finalised blocks, a GPU prover market, "the proof that covers the most wins", proofs bound to their maker) and its price: 26 minutes to prove an aggregate of one transfer in a test setup, and at least 512 GB of memory for a production proof. Canggu built the core (one SP1 proof per batch) and measured the same lesson. See Signature compression.

Is verifiable useful here?

verifiable proves "I am one of these registered members" without saying which one. It uses ring VRFs on the Bandersnatch curve, with 752-byte proofs and one unlinkable 32-byte alias per member per context.

  • Useful for front-running metadata. A sealed commitment could carry a ring proof that it came from some registered account, with one alias per epoch as a rate limit, instead of going through a relayer.
  • Not useful for signature compression. It provides anonymity, not aggregation; its proofs are larger than a plain signature and do not combine.
  • Not post-quantum. Its security rests on elliptic curves. Its interface, though, could front a post-quantum implementation (for example a STARK over a Merkle tree with nullifiers, which is how Rand's shielded pool works).

It was evaluated but not used in the code.
